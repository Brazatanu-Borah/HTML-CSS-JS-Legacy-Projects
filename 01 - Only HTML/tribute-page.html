✨ Marie Curie ✨

1867 - 1934

Pioneer of Radioactivity · First Woman to Win a Nobel Prize

🌍 Her Enduring Legacy

Maria Salomea Skłodowska-Curie, born in Warsaw, Poland, devoted her life to scientific discovery. She co-discovered polonium and radium, introduced the scientific term radioactivity, and carried out groundbreaking research that revolutionized both physics and medicine.

Despite facing significant barriers as a woman in 19th-century academia, she became the first person in history to win two Nobel Prizes— receiving the Nobel Prize in Physics (1903) and later the Nobel Prize in Chemistry (1911). She remains the only individual to receive Nobel Prizes in two different scientific fields.

During World War I, she developed mobile radiography units, affectionately called "Petites Curies", which brought X-ray technology directly to battlefield hospitals, helping doctors diagnose injuries quickly and saving countless lives.

📅 Life Timeline

  • 1867 — Born in Warsaw, Poland (then part of the Russian Empire).
  • 1891 — Moves to Paris to study mathematics and physics at the Sorbonne.
  • 1895 — Marries Pierre Curie and begins scientific collaboration.
  • 1898 — Discovers the elements polonium and radium.
  • 1903 — Wins the Nobel Prize in Physics alongside Pierre Curie and Henri Becquerel.
  • 1906 — Becomes the first female professor at the Sorbonne following Pierre's death.
  • 1911 — Wins the Nobel Prize in Chemistry.
  • 1914-1918 — Develops mobile X-ray units for the French Army during World War I.
  • 1934 — Dies from aplastic anemia after years of radiation exposure.

🔬 Scientific Contributions

Radioactivity
Defined and named the phenomenon of radioactivity, establishing an entirely new scientific discipline.
Radium & Polonium
Discovered these two elements and investigated their unique properties, laying the groundwork for radiation-based medical treatments.
Medical Innovation
Developed portable X-ray units that dramatically improved emergency medical care during wartime.
Nobel Legacy
Inspired generations of physicists, chemists, physicians, and researchers through groundbreaking discoveries.

💡 Did You Know?

  • 📖 Marie Curie's notebooks remain radioactive today and are stored inside lead-lined boxes.
  • 🏛️ She became the first woman honored in the Panthéon in Paris based solely on her own achievements.
  • 🏅 Her daughter, Irène Joliot-Curie, also received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1935.
  • ⚛️ The unit of radioactivity known as the curie (Ci) was named in honor of Marie and Pierre Curie.
